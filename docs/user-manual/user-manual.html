How to read this manual

It assumes you know nothing about Chhatri. Every new word is explained the first time it appears, and there is a glossary at the end. If you only have five minutes, read section 1, then the three-minute story in section 7.

1What Chhatri is

Chhatri (छतरी, Hindi for "umbrella") is income cover for India's small merchants. When something stops a shop from earning, such as a red rain alert that empties the street, Chhatri notices the loss in the shop's own sales, pays the same day, explains the amount in Hindi, and asks the shop's lender to give that day's loan instalment a holiday. The merchant does not fill in a form. The claim starts itself. The AI builds the case and a rules engine written in ordinary code decides the money. Every step goes into a log that shows if anyone changed it.

The one-line version

Rain stops sales. Paytm sees the drop. Chhatri pays that evening, with no form and no phone call, and the lender decides about the instalment.

2The problem, and who it is for

The problem

A tea stall earns day by day. On a day of heavy rain nobody stops for tea, and the stall's sales can fall by more than half. The owner still has to pay his loan instalment. Illness does the same: if the owner is in hospital, the shop is shut and no money comes in.

Insurance exists for such days, but it is slow. The facts file for this project records that earlier merchant protection plans on Paytm took 30 to 60 days per claim and needed several documents (fact A3). A merchant living on daily sales cannot wait that long.

The story used in the demo shows the gap. Anil runs a tea stall in Parel, Mumbai. His usual Tuesday sales are ₹4,380. On the storm day his area's sales fall 63%. His loan instalment of ₹600 is still due. With Chhatri he is paid ₹1,380 the same evening and his lender is asked to pause the instalment.

Who uses Chhatri

The merchant

A shop owner such as Anil. Uses a phone app in Hindi or English: sees cover, gets paid, asks questions, files a complaint. Does not file claims for a storm.

The claims officer

A person who looks at the doubtful cases: a slip with the wrong name, or a merchant who says the amount is too low. Works in the console on the Claims page.

The lender

The business that gave the merchant the loan. Chhatri only asks it to pause an instalment. The lender decides. In this prototype the lender is simulated.

Why it matters

  • Speed. Money arrives the same evening, not weeks later.
  • No paperwork for a storm. An area claim needs no document at all. A hospital claim needs one photo of the hospital slip.
  • Fairness you can check. Every amount is a sum the merchant can read: half of the usual day, times the drop.
  • Humans stay in charge of doubt. If the AI is not sure, a person decides.

3How it works

Follow one claim from left to right. A trigger (something that starts a claim) goes to the policy engine, which reads a rules file called rules.yaml. If the rules say yes, money is paid and the lender is asked about the instalment. Everything is written to the audit log.

The two kinds of claim

Area claim (storm)Personal claim (illness)
What starts itA red or orange weather alert, and the hourly sales index of a zone stays below 50% of expected for 3 hours in a row, with at least 20 shops in the zone.A shop shows a full day with no sales. Chhatri checks in on WhatsApp in Hindi. The merchant sends one photo of the hospital slip.
DocumentsNone.One hospital slip.
AmountHalf of the lost sales, capped at ₹2,500 per shop per day.Half of the usual day, capped at ₹1,500 a day, up to 3 days paid automatically.
Example½ × ₹4,380 × 63% = ₹1,380 (Anil).½ × ₹4,300 = ₹2,150, capped at ₹1,500 for 1 day = ₹1,500.
The key words

Expected sales: what the shop normally sells at this hour, estimated by a small statistical model. Sales index: actual sales divided by expected sales. Zone: a group of neighbouring shops in one area; the demo uses Z3, Z7, Z9 and Z12. Settlement: the daily payment Paytm makes to a merchant. EDI: the equated daily instalment of a loan, taken out of the settlement. EDI holiday: a pause of one instalment, which moves to the end of the loan.

Who is allowed to decide what

CaseChhatri aloneGoes to a human
Area drop during an alert, index clearPaysOnly if the merchant disputes

Slip unclear or dates do not matchNeverAlways

This is the table on the Policy page (screen 6.13). Other rules in rules.yaml, version pilot-0.1: payout share 50%; slip name must match the KYC name (score 85 or more); slip reading confidence 0.80 or more; waiting period 7 days; annual limit ₹30,000; disputes answered within 24 hours. KYC means the identity record of the merchant.

The rule that matters

The AI builds the case. Code decides the money. A message that tells a merchant an amount is filled from a fixed template with numbers from the engine, never written freely by a model.

4What is real and what is simulated

Chhatri is a prototype, and it says so on screen. Every outside service has a small label in the console header (click the chip that reads Simulated). There are three labels:

LabelMeaning
LIVEA real service is being called, because you set its key. Only AI services can be live here.
SIMULATEDA simulator answers instead. Nothing outside is called.
FALLBACKThe service was meant to be live but failed or was switched off, so a simpler answer is used. This label appears with the provider panel flag.
PartStatus in this prototype
Policy engine, forecast model, audit log, claims consoleReal code, running for real (on simulated shop data).
Shop sales, weather alerts feed, KYC, payout rail, lender, Soundbox (the speaker on the till)Always simulated and always labelled.
WhatsAppSimulated. The phone you see is drawn inside the console. It becomes live only with four WhatsApp settings, which this project has not run.
Paytm payment linkSimulated. A pretend link such as https://paytm.me/sim-... is shown and labelled.
Sarvam (speech, chat, slip reading)Simulated unless SARVAM_API_KEY is set.
Gemini (chat and slip reading)Simulated unless GOOGLE_API_KEY and GEMINI_MODEL are set.
Workflows (n8n)An in-process runner does the same steps. The n8n label reads simulated unless you run the Docker stack.
WeatherRain data in the backtest is real, from Open-Meteo, saved in the repository. The live weather call is off by default.
Honest limits
  • AI services run only on synthetic demo data. The setting CHHATRI_DATA_IS_SYNTHETIC=true is the declaration. Without it, every free-tier AI call is skipped.
  • The backtest (section 6.12) uses simulated sales with real rainfall. It tests the rule, not accuracy on real shops. Its simulation was tuned so the demo numbers appear, and premiums are priced so each zone ends near a 65% loss ratio, so those numbers are by design, not a finding.
  • No insurer, lender or Paytm team has agreed to take part. Nothing here is an approved insurance product.
  • No measured accuracy for any live AI model exists yet. The evaluation page says NOT MEASURED until a run is stored.

5Install and run

What you need first

  • Python 3.12 and Node 22 (Vite, the web tool, needs Node 20.19 or newer) and GNU make.
  • Docker with Compose v2, only if you use make up.
  • No API key is needed. Without keys every service is simulated.

Run everything on your machine

git clone <repo> chhatri && cd chhatri
make setup     # backend/.venv with the backend, and npm ci in frontend/
make env       # creates .env with a generated secret (never overwrites an existing one)
make dev       # backend on http://localhost:8000, console on http://localhost:5173 (Ctrl+C stops both)

Open http://localhost:5173. The backend loads the monsoon scenario at start, paused at 08:00. GET http://localhost:8000/api/preflight lists what is ready. make data rebuilds the saved model files; it is slow and needed only if a file is missing, because the results are committed.

Run with no backend at all (the static mock)

The console can run a copy of the whole backend inside the browser. Nothing else needs to run, so it works offline and can be put on a free static host.

cd frontend
npm ci
npm run dev:mock                                   # development server in mock mode

# a static build, shown exactly with the flags it was built with:
VITE_FEATURES=n1_miniapp npm run build -- --mode mock
npm run preview                                      # then open /merchant/S-0142/app

In mock mode every service is simulated and the footer shows a "Mock data" badge. A full page reload restarts the mock, so move around with the links, not the reload button.

The 14 feature flags

A feature flag is a switch. Every feature added for the final is off until you name it. Off means absent: the screen is hidden and the backend answers an ordinary 404. Set the same list in two places: CHHATRI_FEATURES for the backend (in .env) and VITE_FEATURES for the console, separated by commas.

CHHATRI_FEATURES=n1_miniapp,n2_ask_chhatri,n3_slip_precheck,n4_voice,n5_grievances,n6_consents,n8_marathi,x4_lender_request,x6_provider_panel,x8_distress_guard,h8_ops_strip,h24_whatif,h25_evals,console_polish

6.7 The what-if drawer

Zone card · button What if... · flag h24_whatif

A read-only calculator for judges. It re-runs the real trigger rule for Zone 7 with numbers you change. Nothing is saved and nobody is paid.

6.22 Slip pre-check

Tab Help · Send your hospital slip · scenario illness, Thursday 21 Aug 11:20

The personal claim. Chhatri noticed Anil's shop had no sales yesterday and asked if he is well. Now he sends one photo of the hospital slip. The app reads it and shows what it found before any check runs. Anil must confirm.

  1. Press Take a photo or Choose from the gallery. For the demo, press Use the demo slip (or Use a blurry demo slip to see a bad photo handed back).
  2. If asked, tick the consent for reading the slip.

6.23 Ask Chhatri, with citations

Tab Help · Ask Chhatri

A question box. The answer is built from the engine's own facts and cites the policy clause it relies on. At the bottom of an answer a small label says what wrote it (SIMULATED, FALLBACK or LIVE).

  1. Tap an example question, such as Why did I get this amount?, or type your own and press Send.
  2. Read the answer: formula, then From the policy with a clause chip such as C4.1 How much we pay, then Based on with the numbers and their source badges.
  3. Tap the clause chip to read the clause in a sheet (next picture).
  4. If a message looks like a scam (asks for an OTP or an advance fee), a warning banner appears.

6.24 Voice chips

Ask Chhatri · Speak (shown in Hindi) · flag n4_voice

  3. Check the text. Tap Yes on the amount chip, or Change to correct it.

6.25 Grievances

Tab Help · Complaints and escalation · flag n5_grievances

Where a merchant complains. With nothing filed, it says so. After filing it shows one ladder: the claims officer (you are here, we reply within 24 hours), then the insurer's grievance officer, then IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal, then the Insurance Ombudsman. The outside steps are labelled SIMULATED or "file it yourself".

  1. Press New complaint.
  2. Pick a topic, for example The amount of a payout, then write a few words (up to 500 characters).
  3. Press Send. The ladder appears with the case number.
  4. Press Mark as solved to close it.

6.26 Consent centre and activity

Tab Help · My data and consent · flag n6_consents

Consent is the merchant's permission to use data for one purpose. Each purpose has a switch (use my sales to decide claims, read my hospital slip), the date it was agreed, and a What we use note. The right picture is the activity log: what was used, for what and when.

  1. Turn a switch off. A sheet explains the effect, for example that Chhatri stops reading slips. Confirm or keep it.
  2. Press See what was used and when to open the activity log. Filter by Sales, Slip or Premium.
  3. Press Check the log to verify nothing was changed.
  4. If a slip is held, press Erase this slip to delete it on request.

6.27 Help

Tab Help

One list of everything else: what am I covered for, Ask Chhatri, complaints, my data and consent, language, and send your hospital slip. A line explains that if an amount looks wrong you open the claim and tap This is wrong. Insurance words are listed with the jargon lens.

  1. Tap a row to open it. Back returns here.
  2. Tap a word such as Weather alert to see it explained.

Questions a newcomer asks

Is Chhatri a real insurance product?
No. It is a hackathon prototype. No insurer, lender or Paytm team has agreed to take part, and nothing here is an approved product.
Does real money move?
No. Payouts, the lender and the payment link are simulated and labelled.
Who decides the money, the AI or the code?
The code. A policy engine reads rules.yaml and decides. The AI only builds the case and explains.
What happens when the AI is unsure or unavailable?
A doubtful slip goes to a claims officer, never to an automatic payout. If an AI service is not available, a simple template or simulator answers and the label says so.
How is the payout worked out?
For a storm, half of the lost sales: ½ × the usual day × the drop. For Anil, ½ × ₹4,380 × 63% = ₹1,380. For illness, half of the usual day, capped at ₹1,500 a day.
Why did Zone 9 get nothing?
Its sales fell to 61% on a day with no weather alert. That is a slow day, not a loss event.
Does Chhatri pause a loan?
No. It asks the lender, under a rule agreed in advance, and the lender decides. In the demo the simulated lender says yes.
Why can a merchant not buy cover when a storm is forecast?
Cover starts after a 7-day waiting period, with a 72-hour look-ahead at alerts. Otherwise people would buy cover only on the eve of a storm.
Do I need API keys or an internet connection?
No. Without keys everything is simulated, and the static mock runs in the browser with no backend.
Which languages does it speak?
Hindi and English. Marathi is a draft, in text only, behind n8_marathi. Voice is Hindi and English.
Is any real person's data used?
No. Every merchant, slip and sales figure is synthetic. AI services are called only when that is declared.
How can I check that nobody changed the log?
Open the Audit page and press Verify chain, or press Check the log on a receipt in the mini-app.

Glossary

TermMeaning
AlertA weather warning from the India Meteorological Department with colours: Green, Yellow, Orange, Red. Chhatri looks at Red and Orange.
Audit logA record of every step. Each entry holds the fingerprint of the one before, so a change is detected.
BacktestA replay of past monsoons to see what the rules would have paid. Here it uses simulated sales and real rainfall.
Basis riskThe gap between what the trigger says and what the merchant really lost.
CaseAn open dispute or doubtful claim, with a 24-hour answer clock, such as C-2291.
ClaimA request to be paid after a loss.
ConsentA permission to use your data for one named purpose. You can withdraw it.
CounterfactualA line that says what would have changed the outcome.
EDIEquated daily instalment: the daily loan repayment taken from a merchant's settlement.
EDI holidayA pause of one instalment, which moves to the end of the loan. Chhatri asks; the lender decides.
Expected salesWhat a shop normally sells at that hour or on that weekday, estimated by a model.
Feature flagA switch that is off until you turn it on.
Hospital cashA fixed daily amount for each day in hospital, whatever the bill.
IRDAIThe Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India. Its Bima Bharosa portal takes insurance complaints.
Jargon lensTap an insurance word to see a plain explanation.
KYCKnow Your Customer: the identity record of the merchant. A slip's name must match it.
LenderThe business that lent the merchant money. It decides about an EDI holiday.
ParametricInsurance that pays when a set trigger happens, without measuring each shop's loss.
PayoutMoney paid after a claim is approved.
Policy engineThe code that decides APPROVED, REFERRED or DECLINED. Only it can approve money.
REFERREDA decision that sends the claim to a person.
ReplayA run of a past day, with a clock you control.
rules.yamlThe file that holds the policy rules and numbers.
Sales indexActual sales divided by expected sales for a zone.
SettlementThe daily payment from Paytm to a merchant, after fees and loan deductions.
SlipThe hospital paper (admission or discharge) used as proof for a hospital claim.
SoundboxThe speaker on a merchant's till that announces payments.
Source badgeA small label that says where a number came from and when.
TriggerThe event that starts a claim.
Waiting periodThe days after buying cover before it can pay. Here, 7 days.
ZoneA group of neighbouring shops in one area. The demo uses Z3, Z7, Z9 and Z12.
